Emotional Regulation vs. Suppression (10 mins)

Suppression (Bottle up)

Pushing emotions down, pretending they don't exist. Increases physiological stress and leads to explosive outbursts later.

Regulation (Process)

Acknowledging the emotion, managing bodily responses, and choosing a constructive, healthy path forward.

Stage II: Coping Toolkit & Grounding Practice

Individual Practice

Explore and test four distinct evidence-based emotional regulation strategies in your personal coping log.

1. Box Breathing Technique

4-4-4-4 Cycle:

  • Inhale through nose for 4 seconds.
  • Hold breath for 4 seconds.
  • Exhale slowly through mouth for 4 seconds.
  • Hold empty for 4 seconds. Repeat 3 times.

2. 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding

To pull out of panic/overwhelm, identify around you:

5 things you see • 4 things you touch • 3 things you hear • 2 things you smell • 1 positive thing about yourself.

3. Control vs. No Control Audit

Circle what you CAN control (your effort, response, boundaries) and release what you CANNOT (others' reactions, past events).

4. Activating Support Network

Identify 2 Trusted Adults (e.g., School Counselor, Parent, Coach, Teacher) to contact when stress exceeds self-help strategies.

Stage III: Fictional Stress Case Analysis

Teams of 3 - 4
Case Study: Maya's Overload

Maya is balancing 3 AP assignments, varsity sports practice, and family responsibilities. She hasn't slept well in 4 days. When a teammate gently asks if she finished her part of the report, Maya bursts into tears, yells "Stop pressuring me!" and locks herself in the restroom.

Team Task (10 mins):

  1. Identify Maya's cumulative stressors vs. her acute trigger.
  2. Was Maya regulating or suppressing her emotions leading up to this moment? Explain.
  3. Design a Rescue Coping Plan for Maya utilizing at least two techniques from Stage II.

Montano Roles

  • Facilitator: Manages time (10m) & ensures equal voices.
  • Scribe: Records the team's Rescue Coping Plan.
  • Reporter: Shares 1 key insight during Plenary.
